Big Bazaar philosophy
Big Bazaar’s core philosophy was to understand the Indian consumer as they actually shop, rather than copy Western retail formats. Kishore Biyani’s famous formulation was:

“Retail is our religion, the consumer is our God, and stores are our temples.”

The philosophy can be summarised as:

1. Consumer first
Big Bazaar believed the consumer—not management, competitors, or investors—ultimately decides whether a business succeeds. What to sell, how to sell it, which format to use, and how stores should evolve were supposed to come from observing customers.

2. Make aspiration affordable
Its purpose was to give ordinary Indian households access to the variety, experience, and dignity previously associated with affluent shopping. Biyani described the broader ambition as providing “the ordinary people what only the rich can afford.”

This was expressed through the positioning:

“Isse sasta aur achha kahin nahin” — nothing cheaper and better anywhere else.

Price was not merely a promotion; it was central to the brand promise. The business attempted to support this through scale, high transaction volumes, private labels, promotions, and a low-margin, high-turnover model.

3. Indian, not imported
Big Bazaar did not try to reproduce a perfectly organised Western supermarket. It borrowed from Indian bazaars, mandis, neighbourhood stores, and family retail businesses.

The stores were intentionally busy, crowded, colourful, promotional, and highly merchandise-led. Biyani’s view was that apparent “chaos” could feel familiar and comfortable to Indian shoppers, whereas copying international retail environments could make the store feel alien.

4. Shopping as an experience
Biyani believed shopping involved discovery, human interaction, touch, and emotion—not only efficient transactions. Big Bazaar therefore mixed:

Product variety.

Bargain hunting.

Seasonal events.

Family shopping.

Visual merchandising.

Touch-and-feel product interaction.

In-store promotions.

A bazaar-like atmosphere.

Shopping was meant to become an occasion rather than a purely functional activity.

5. Build for Indian diversity
Future Group’s philosophy was based on the idea that India is not one homogeneous market. Different communities, regions, income groups, and cultural habits require different products, formats, and experiences.

This led to a broader Future Group approach of developing businesses from Indian consumer insights and creating environments in which consumers could comfortably buy according to their own habits.

The philosophy in one sentence
Observe Indian consumers deeply, create familiar but modern shopping experiences, offer aspiration at accessible prices, and let consumer behaviour determine the business.

How it connects to Think9
Think9 appears to inherit this philosophy but apply it to a newer environment. Big Bazaar applied consumer understanding primarily through stores, formats, merchandising, price, and retail scale. Think9 applies it through consumer research, digital brands, cultural insight, product development, content, venture building, and omnichannel distribution.
